# 01 — Vision

> **Kills fuel evolution. Returns fuel tactical choice. Dominate the map.**
> *Evolve to dominate: from Base Form to Ascendant roster.*

## Pitch

**Greyborn** is a **4v4 team PvP game** about evolution and territory, set
on the planet **Greyborn**. Every player spawns as a small humanoid **Base Form**,
a native creature of the planet, and grows during the match,
through 20 levels and 3 Stages, into a towering **Ascendant** monster. The
lineages are Titan, Brawler, Verdant, Hollow, Thornrunner and Bonespire.

You evolve by **killing** (collect evolution cores), by **destroying enemy
Cores** (deny their evolution) and by **returning to base** to convert what you
carry. Returning is risky but strategic. Meanwhile your team **roots** the
map's nodes, controls **Resource Hubs** and grows **Territorial Influence**.
Ascendants are built to depend on each other: paired lineages unlock
**Synergies** that neither can do alone.

**The war:** Greyborn is alive and aware. Long ago a meteorite, the
**Starwound**, struck it, carrying a hive-mind infection, the **Murmur**. Every
match is one battle between the planet's **Wildborn** (nature's response) and
the Murmur's **Blightborn** (the infected), fought over the planet's own body.
See [10 — The World](10-world.md).

## Design pillars

1. **Evolution Is the Scoreboard.** A player's size, silhouette and sound
   *are* their progress. You can read the state of a fight by looking at it:
   a Level 20 Titan is visibly, audibly a different threat from a Level 8 one.
2. **Risk What You Carry.** Cores you carry are progress you haven't banked.
   Every return to base is a decision: bank now and lose map pressure, or push
   your luck for a bigger conversion bonus.
3. **Interdependency Over Individuals.** The strongest moments need two
   Ascendants committing together (Synergies). A team that coordinates beats
   four strong solo players.
4. **The Map Is Alive.** Territory grows, nodes open and close, borders
   create Tension, and Tension triggers global events. The map in minute 18
   doesn't look like the map in minute 2.

## What the player feels over a match

| Minutes | Fantasy | Main activity |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Small, fast, vulnerable | Root first nodes, skirmish for early cores, awaken your lineage (Level 3) |
| 3–10 | Growing predator | Fights over Resource Hubs, first base returns, Stage 2 at Level 10 (choose a branch) |
| 10–18 | Pack of monsters | Synergies come online (Levels 12/15/18), Enemy Core sieges, global events |
| 18–end | Titans at war | Level 20 Ultimate Forms, Hunt phase, final push on the enemy base |

## The experience in detail

Imagine a first match through a player's eyes.

You begin as something small. A grey creature, knee-high to a stag, blinking
in the light of a birth-node that has just split open. Three others stand
beside you: your brood. Nothing on the screen asks you to fight yet. You hear
wind, birds, the creak of trees, and somewhere far off the low rumble of a
herd. Overhead, a glowing scar crosses the sky. You don't know what it is,
but the world seems to be watching it.

Within minutes you've eaten your first cores: a skink, a darting fox, an
enemy Kith that wandered too far from its brood. Something in your back
aches. At Level 3 it breaks open, and you are no longer a Kith but the first
shape of a Titan, or a Verdant, or a Thornrunner. Across the map, you hear
the enemy changing too.

By the middle of the match you're carrying hundreds of cores, and you can
*hear yourself*: your footsteps heavier, your aura humming. Every enemy
nearby can hear you too. You make a decision every player in Greyborn makes
dozens of times: go home now, safe but slower, or take one more fight. You
choose the fight, win it, and run home with your heart pounding. At the
birth-pool you kneel, the cores pour out of you as light, and your body
swells.

By the end you are enormous. Your steps shake the ground. You and your
partner wait for each other's signal, and when the slam lands and the roll
follows, the enemy Verdant flies. The map is a patchwork of amber and cyan.
The world has changed around you: nodes taken and lost, herds scattered, a
volcano's lava cutting a fresh line through the territory. You win, or you
lose, and either way the planet takes it in: roots flood the enemy base, or
crystal glazes your own.

That is the experience Greyborn sells: **growing from nothing into something
the whole map fears, in a world that is alive around you.**

## The pillars in practice

**1. Evolution Is the Scoreboard.** Most competitive games show progress in
numbers. Greyborn shows it in bodies. A spectator who has never played can
watch a match and understand who is winning by looking at the size of the
creatures. This is also why sound signatures matter: power has a voice, and
that voice is also a weakness.

**2. Risk What You Carry.** Every core you carry is unbanked progress, and
every return is a gamble. This turns every player's movement into a story:
the run home with 300 cores is tense in a way a simple "experience bar" can
never be. It also creates hunting: the most valuable target on the map is
often not the strongest creature, but the heaviest-laden one.

**3. Interdependency Over Individuals.** Synergies, cross-resonance and
Stillheart's Attunement make every lineage stronger beside a partner. A
team of four skilled players who don't coordinate will lose to four average
players who call their slams and marks. The game rewards friends.

**4. The Map Is Alive.** Wildlife hunts and flees, weather changes, borders
build Tension, patrons intervene, and the War Map carries the results of
every season into the next. No two matches on the same map play out quite alike.

## Who Greyborn is for

| Player | What they get |
|---|---|
| **Competitive team players** | Draft depth (pairs, cross-resonance, branches), readable mirror balance, a ranked ladder |
| **Players who love monsters and growth** | A visible transformation from small creature to giant, every match |
| **Friends who play together** | Pairs, Pair Bonds, 2v2 Brood Skirmish, voice-free coordination through calls |
| **Lore and world lovers** | A wordless story told through Memories, seasons and a living planet |
| **Casual players** | Apex Hunt, world events, AI matches and a forgiving onboarding |

## What Greyborn is not

- **Not a lane-based MOBA.** There are no minions or lanes; territory and wildlife replace them.
- **Not pay-to-win.** Nothing outside a match gives power inside one ([26](26-collection.md)).
- **Not a story told in words.** The world never speaks; it shows.
- **Not good against evil.** The planet defends itself and the Murmur is a
  refugee from a dead world ([48](48-beyond-the-sky.md)). Both sides are worth playing.

## What makes Greyborn different, in one breath

**Every player grows from a small creature into a giant during the match,
on a living planet at war with an alien mind, where carrying your progress
home is the biggest risk you take and your partner is your greatest strength.**

## The promise to players

Greyborn makes players five promises:

1. **You will grow.** Every match, from a small creature to a giant.
2. **Your choices will matter.** When to return, which branch to take, which partner to stay beside.
3. **The world will be alive.** Herds, weather, events and a story that moves every season.
4. **The fight will be fair.** Both sides are always equal; nothing is for sale that wins matches.
5. **You'll be part of something larger.** Every match feeds the war, and the war writes the world's history.

## The emotional range of one match

A good Greyborn match should move players through a range of feelings:

| Moment | Feeling |
|---|---|
| Budding | Calm, curiosity |
| Early hunting | Playfulness |
| Lineage awakening | Excitement |
| Carrying 300 cores home | Tension, dread |
| Converting at the birth-pool | Relief, power |
| A Synergy landing | Elation, connection with your partner |
| A patron event | Awe |
| A lost fight | Frustration, but never humiliation |
| Phase 4 as an Ascendant | Majesty |
| The ending | Triumph, or dignified loss |

## The design tensions at Greyborn's heart

Great games are built on tensions that never fully resolve. Greyborn has four:

1. **Risk vs. safety.** Carry more for a bigger bonus, or return now?
2. **Individual vs. pair.** Roam for kills alone, or stay with your partner for Synergies?
3. **Fight vs. territory.** Chase the enemy, or hold the map?
4. **Planet vs. Murmur, and both vs. the Clamor.** In the story, who is the enemy, really?

Each match asks the first three. Each season asks the fourth.

## Why monsters, and why a planet

**Why monsters?** Because transformation is one of the most satisfying
fantasies in games, and monsters let that transformation be physical,
visible and huge. A player doesn't just get stronger; they become something else.

**Why a living planet?** Because it makes the map more than a board. When the
world itself is a character (one that breathes, sleeps, fights and feels pain),
every match happens inside a story, and every node captured is a nerve of something alive.

**Why a hive mind as the enemy?** Because it can copy the planet's creatures,
which justifies both teams sharing a roster, and because a mind that can only
copy is a mystery waiting to be solved, which gives the story somewhere to go.

## Inspirations beyond games

The tone draws on sources outside games:

- **Nature documentaries:** herds, migrations, predators and prey, the patience of the natural world.
- **Myth and folklore about the land as a living being:** mountains as sleeping giants, forests that breathe.
- **Wordless storytelling** in animation and illustration, where image and music carry everything.
- **Stories of refugees and lost homes,** for the Murmur's arc.
- **The changing of the seasons,** for the planet's year.

## What a spectator sees

Greyborn is designed to be watchable. A spectator who has never played
should be able to follow a match because:

- **Size shows who's winning.** The bigger creatures belong to the team with more evolution.
- **Colour shows who holds the map.** Amber and cyan spread across the ground as territory changes.
- **Sound shows the big moments.** Stage changes, Synergies and patron events are heard map-wide.
- **The world reacts.** Stampedes, falling stars and erupting volcanoes make every match look different.
- **The risk is visible.** A glowing, humming carrier running home is a story anyone can follow.

## Closing statement

Greyborn is a game about growing up into something huge, on a world that is
alive, in a war where no one is simply wrong. It asks players to take risks,
to trust a partner, and to listen to a world that never speaks. If it does
that well, players won't just remember the matches they won. They'll
remember the planet.

## The vision in five words

**Grow. Risk. Pair. Listen. Remember.** Grow from a Kith into an Ascendant;
risk what you carry; fight beside your pair; listen to a living world; and
remember the planet's story, season after season.
