# 01 — Vision & Pillars

## Elevator pitch

> In a world whose colour was sung away, every living thing carries a single
> **Hue** — a soul-colour that is also its magic. You were born **Greyborn**:
> hueless, powerless, and hunted as an omen. But what has no colour can hold
> *any* colour. Tear Hues from the things you kill, wear their power for a
> while, and decide how much of yourself you are willing to stain to bring
> colour back to the world.

**Genre:** 2D hand-drawn action-adventure (metroidvania structure, soulslike combat weight)
**Platforms (target):** PC (Steam) first; Switch / consoles as a follow-up
**Session shape:** 20–40 minute sessions; full run ~18–25 hours, ~35 for completion
**Rating target:** T / PEGI 16 (dark themes, stylised violence, no gore focus)

## The hook in one mechanic

**You have no moveset of your own.** Your base kit is a plain grey blade, a
dodge and a parry. Everything else comes from **Hues** you rip from enemies:
take a Red from a burning hound and your blade ignites and your dodge becomes a
flame-dash; take a Blue from a drowned knight and you parry with a tide-shield.

Hues are powerful but **temporary and dangerous** — the longer you hold one, the
more it **Stains** you (see [03 — Core Gameplay](03-core-gameplay.md)). Every
fight asks: *spend it now, bank it, or let it go before it changes you?*

## Design pillars

1. **Borrowed Power, Real Cost**
   Every strong option is taken from the world and leaves a mark. Power is never
   free; the player always knows what it costs them.
2. **Colour Is Information**
   The world is greyscale by default. Colour on screen *always* means something:
   a Hue to steal, a threat, a secret, a path. Art and gameplay share one language.
3. **Read, Then Answer**
   Combat is about reading an enemy's colour-coded tells and answering with the
   right borrowed tool. Deliberate, punishing, fair — never button mashing.
4. **A World That Remembers Its Colour**
   Restoring Hues to the land permanently changes it: new paths, returning NPCs,
   altered enemies. Progress is visible on the map and in the environment itself.

### Anti-pillars (what Greyborn is *not*)

- Not a loot game — no randomised gear stats, no rarity tiers.
- Not a roguelike — death costs progress, not the run.
- Not a power fantasy that ends in godhood — the strongest endings demand restraint.

## Target audience

- Primary: players of *Hollow Knight*, *Blasphemous*, *Ender Lilies*, *Salt and Sanctuary*.
- Secondary: players who loved *Dark Souls* build-craft but want a 2D, shorter, tighter game.
- Motivations served: mastery (combat), discovery (interconnected world),
  meaningful choice (Stain / endings).

## Comparables & where we differ

| Game | We share | We differ |
|---|---|---|
| Hollow Knight | Interconnected map, melancholic tone | Movesets are stolen & temporary, not permanently unlocked |
| Ender Lilies | Gaining powers from defeated foes | Powers decay and corrupt; using them is a resource decision |
| Blasphemous | Dark religious-tinged world, weighty combat | Colour-restoration changes the world state visibly |
| Okami | Colour restoration as progress | Colour is *taken*, not painted — the protagonist is the thief |

## Unique selling points (store-page bullets)

- **Steal your enemies' souls — literally.** 12 Hues, 36+ stolen abilities, combinable in pairs.
- **A greyscale world you paint back to life** — every restored region transforms.
- **Stain:** a corruption system that changes your body, your abilities, and the ending.
- **Hand-drawn art where colour is reserved for meaning.**

## Success criteria for the vertical slice

- A new player can explain the Hue/Stain trade-off after 15 minutes without a tooltip.
- Playtesters voluntarily discard a Hue at least once to avoid Stain.
- Boss fight #1 has a median of 4–8 attempts (challenging, not a wall).
