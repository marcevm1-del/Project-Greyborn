# 39 — World Events

Short, recurring events that make the world feel alive between the big
season beats. Each is a **weekend or 3-day event** that changes atmosphere,
wildlife or rewards, **never the balance between sides**. All are proposals.

## Rules for world events

1. **Fair to both sides.** Every change applies equally to Wildborn and Blightborn.
2. **Readable.** Every event shows a clear sky or weather change at match start, so players know it's on.
3. **Rooted in the world.** Every event is something that would really happen on Greyborn: a migration, a storm, a song.
4. **Not in ranked** by default, except purely visual events, to keep competitive play consistent.
5. **Front points** ([18](18-the-answering-war.md)) still count during events, so the war keeps moving.

## The events in depth

Each event should feel like a natural happening on a living planet, not a
playlist rule. Here is what each one looks, sounds and feels like.

**The Great Migration.** For a weekend, every open map is crossed by herds far
bigger than usual. On Ashfall Crossing the migration runs twice as often; on
the Ash Sea and the Ashen Steppe maps, smaller herds cut across every route.
The ground rumbles constantly. Mossback Grazers, Rimeback Stags and Stiltwalkers
move together, and predators (Ashfang packs) shadow them on the flanks. Players
learn to fight *around* the herds: a stampede can split a team, cover a
retreat, or carry a careless Base Form away. The event ends with a Memory
unlock showing the herds following the Hunt That Never Ended.

**Starfall Nights.** Every match is played at night under a sky full of
falling meteor glass. Shard Storms are frequent, and every falling shard
lights the ground cyan-violet for a moment before it fades. Starshard veins
give double cores, so the crater zones become the busiest places on the map.
The music is quieter and stranger: glass tones over a slow heartbeat. Players
remember Starfall weekends for their look: dark maps lit only by the Lantern
moon, Blight's glow and falling stars.

**Moth Bloom.** Dusk on every map. As the light fades, clouds of Ember Moths
rise from the grass in slow spirals. They're harmless and easy to eat, but
anyone standing inside a cloud is outlined in soft gold and revealed. It's a
gentle event with a hidden edge: the most beautiful places on the map are
also the most exposed.

**Calving Days.** Mossback Calves appear on every map, tottering after their
herds. Harming one turns the whole herd territorial for a minute. The event
asks players to be careful in a world that is usually only a battlefield.
Wildborn and Blightborn players alike earn the event reward for *not* harming
calves across a set number of matches. It's the only event that rewards restraint.

**Sap Rain.** Warm amber rain falls on every map. SAP income rises by 20% for
both sides, roots spread a little faster in the wet soil, and every surface
glistens. The rain makes Blight look different too: crystal streaked with
running sap, which artists can use as one of the rare images of the two
materials touching peacefully.

**The Long Cold.** Rime Fog and frost on every map. Rimewastes variants
(Frostfangs, Snowback Grazers, Rime Weavers) appear everywhere, and creatures
respawn more slowly. The soundscape drops to near-silence, with ice creaking
and the Sleeper's slow heartbeat underneath. Fights feel slower and tenser.

**Whale Song.** On coastal maps, Stillsong Whales surface every four minutes
and sing. While they sing, Blight stops spreading for 20 seconds, and the
sea goes perfectly still. Everywhere else, the song can be heard faintly on
the wind. The event is a reminder that the planet has old, gentle defences.

**Fever Storms.** Heat haze, red skies at dusk, and predators that attack
more readily. Cinderveil variants appear on every map, and Embergrass fires
spread farther. The event shows the planet's fighting mood: everything is
louder, hotter and more dangerous.

**The Quiet Hours.** Hush Cicada colonies on every map halve all sound
signatures. It's a weekend for ambushes and careful movement. Hollow and
Thornrunner players love it; Titan players learn patience.

**Hunt Weekend.** An apex creature appears in every match, one per phase.
Matches become three-way struggles: the two broods and a Greyback Colossus,
Hive Colossus, Eightfold Matron or Sixfold Wyrm. Apex kills give a unique
trophy mark.

**Landfall Rush.** From Season 3 onward, two Clamor Landfalls per match. The
sky streaks red, Scald spreads from two points, and both teams have to decide
how much to fight the Clamor and how much to fight each other.

## How events are announced

1. **In the world first.** The day before an event, a hint appears on every
   map: distant rumbling before the Migration, red streaks before Landfall
   Rush, a single falling star before Starfall Nights.
2. **On the War Map.** The event shows as weather across the Greyreach.
3. **At match start.** The sky and weather change is clear in the first
   seconds, with a short HUD banner naming the event.

## Checklist for designing new events

- Is it something that would really happen on Greyborn?
- Does it change the same thing for both sides?
- Can a player see it from the sky or weather at match start?
- Does it give a reason to play differently, not just a reward?
- Does it connect to a Turn, a Memory or a season story?

## More world events (#12–#20)

The event list grows to twenty, each rooted in something the planet or its
creatures would really do.

**12 · Elk Sighting (any Turn, weekend).** Heartwood Elk appear in the
Heartwood grove of every map, once per match. Where they stand, roots spread
three times faster for whoever holds the grove. Players of both sides pause to
look. Reward: an Elk-antler Growth for Verdants.

**13 · Old One Crossing (rare, weekend).** One of the Old Ones ([46](46-wild-ascendants.md))
walks across every map in the rotation, slowly, once per match. It can't be
harmed and favours no one; ground it walks on spreads both roots and Blight
faster. Each weekend features a different Old One. Reward: that Old One's Memory.

**14 · The Great Gathering (Ash, 3 days).** Bone Harvestmen across every map
carry bones toward the nearest fossil site. Nests build twice as fast and
hold double cores. Scavengers follow them everywhere. Reward: a Harvestman-bone Marking.

**15 · The Wyrm Stirs (Fever, weekend).** The ground rumbles under every map,
and in Phase 3 the Sixfold Wyrm bursts out of the ground somewhere on the map
(not as a patron event, but as a neutral apex that attacks both teams).
Reward: a Wyrm-scale trophy for the Den.

**16 · Ice Bridges (Rime, 3 days).** On coastal and lake maps, the shallows
freeze into bridges, opening new routes across water for the weekend. Reward:
a Frost-step Instinct (frost spreading from your feet when idle).

**17 · Seed Day (Bloom, weekend).** Seedcallers walk every map, and
everywhere they pass, Sapbloom grows within a minute. A gentle weekend of
healing and regrowth. Reward: a seedling companion for the Den.

**18 · Glimmer Night (any Turn, weekend).** Breathing Night on every map, with
Glimmerfoxes in large numbers; each one killed briefly reveals nearby
enemies. A weekend of hide and seek. Reward: a Glimmer Marking (faint flickering spots).

**19 · Echo Days (Ash, 3 days).** Echo Bells bloom on every map, not just in
the Spirecliffs. Striking them masks footsteps; the whole world rings.
Reward: a Bell call voice.

**20 · Fall-line Watch (any Turn, rare night event).** The Fall-line glows
brighter than ever, and on every map, all creatures (Strays, herds, even
Blighted ones) stop for a few seconds at the same moment and look up. Nothing
else happens. It's a quiet, strange event that hints at the season's story.
Reward: a Watcher emote (looking up at the sky together).

## Event frequency rules

- **One event at a time** in casual queues, so each weekend has a clear identity.
- **No event more than once per season**, except the Great Migration in Ash.
- **At least one event per Turn** that is tied to that Turn's mood.
- **One "quiet" event per season** (Fall-line Watch, Seed Day, Calving Days),
  so the world isn't only ever dangerous.
- **Never during a season finale week.**

## Events and the War Map

Front points still count during events. Some events also add **bonus actions**
that count for fronts:

| Event | Bonus front action |
|---|---|
| The Great Migration | Guiding (Wildborn) or taking (Blightborn) herds through a front's map |
| Calving Days | Protecting calves (both sides) |
| Seed Day | Protecting Seedcaller paths (Wildborn) or seedlings in Blight (Blightborn) |
| Landfall Rush | Destroying Landfall cores (shared, from Season 3) |

## Events in the Den

During an event, the Den shows it: herds passing the crack in the roof during
the Migration, moths drifting in during Moth Bloom, frost on the walls in the
Long Cold, a red glow during Landfall Rush. Players who don't play that
weekend still see the world's event from home.

## Writing an event: an example

**Idea:** *"What if the Stillsea froze?"*
**Rooted in the world?** Yes: the Rimewastes reach the sea, and Rime is the planet's sleep.
**Same for both sides?** Yes: ice bridges open for everyone.
**Visible at match start?** Yes: frozen water and frost in the sky.
**Changes how you play?** Yes: new routes across water.
**Connected to the story?** Yes: Rime is when the Murmur is boldest, and the Sleeper's Memories are clearest.
**Result:** *Ice Bridges* (#16).

## Event audio cues

Every event has a signature sound in its first seconds, so players know
it's on even before they look at the sky:

| Event | Opening sound |
|---|---|
| The Great Migration | Distant rolling thunder of hooves |
| Starfall Nights | A high, glassy streak and a soft chime as a shard lands |
| Calving Days | A calf's call |
| The Long Cold | Ice creaking and the Sleeper's heartbeat |
| Whale Song | Low whale song through the ground |
| Landfall Rush | A far-off roar |
| Old One Crossing | A slow, heavy footstep that shakes the ground once |
| Fall-line Watch | Total silence for one second |

## Event accessibility

- Every event's visual change is paired with its audio cue, and vice versa.
- Events that change visibility (Starfall Nights, Glimmer Night) still keep
  team outlines, weak points and gameplay glows fully readable.
- The HUD banner at match start always names the event in text.

## Why events matter

Events are the world's way of saying *"it's not always the same"*. They
reward players who come back, show off creatures and places that ordinary
matches might not, and keep the planet's year visible week by week.

A season with good events feels like a year on a real planet: migrations, storms,
births, frosts and the occasional strange night when every creature looks up at the sky.

Events are small, but they're how the planet feels lived-in week to week.
