# 33 — Structures of the War

What the map's structures **are** in the world, for each side. The rules
come from [03](03-evolution-system.md) and [05](05-territory-and-economy.md);
names and looks here are proposals.

## Overview

| Game term | Wildborn (the planet) | Blightborn (the Murmur) | What it is in the world |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Base** | **The Cradle** | **The Clutch** | The birth-node cluster where the brood budded ([12](12-life-on-greyborn.md#the-kith-life-cycle)) |
| **Enemy Core** (×3 per team) | **Heartseeds** | **Shard Hearts** | Organs that turn the brood's hunting into growth; destroying one starves the brood's evolution |
| **Resource Hub** (×5) | **Sap Wells** | **Glow Wells** | Places where the planet's blood (SAP) pools; whoever holds one draws on it |
| **Node** | **Root-knot** | **Whisper-node** | A cluster of the planet's nerves, claimed by roots or Blight |
| **Territory** | **Roots** | **Blight** | The planet's nerves or the Murmur's lattice ([10](10-world.md#roots-vs-blight)) |

Hubs are **neutral until claimed**. A Sap Well and a Glow Well are the same
place seen from two sides: when a team captures a Hub, it **regrows** in that
side's material.

## The Cradle / the Clutch (bases)

- **The Cradle (Wildborn):** a ring of huge, split root knots, still
  glistening, around a warm pool of SAP. Fireflies and Lumen Bees drift
  there. Respawning is budding again from a fresh knot.
- **The Clutch (Blightborn):** the same ring of knots, but glazed in glass,
  with cyan light pulsing inside. Whispering is constant. Respawning is a
  crystal pod cracking open.
- **Converting cores** (returning to base, [03](03-evolution-system.md#returning-to-base-conversion))
  looks like **feeding the birth-pool**: the player kneels and the carried
  cores flow out as light into the pool (amber or cyan). The pool glows
  brighter, and the player's body visibly swells with the new growth.
- The **Base Heart** (the target in the proposed win condition, [06](06-match-flow.md#win-conditions-proposal))
  is the **oldest knot** at the centre of the base.

## Heartseeds / Shard Hearts (Enemy Cores)

- **Heartseeds (Wildborn):** a giant seed half-buried in the ground, its husk
  split to show a beating amber heart. Roots run from it toward the Cradle.
- **Shard Hearts (Blightborn):** a geode the size of a house, with a pulsing
  cyan-violet heart inside a crystal shell.
- **When destroyed:** a Heartseed's husk splits and it withers to grey; a
  Shard Heart shatters into glittering dust. Either way, the brood's growth
  slows (−15% passive income per Core, [03](03-evolution-system.md#destroying-enemy-cores-denial)).
- **Regrowth after 4 minutes:** a new seedling (or crystal) rises slowly in
  the same spot, but only if the team still holds territory next to it.

## Sap Wells / Glow Wells (Resource Hubs)

- **Neutral:** an open pool of amber SAP surrounded by a ring of old roots.
- **Held by Wildborn:** the roots thicken into living walls; flowers grow
  around the edge. **Hub Defense levels** (1–10) show as the walls growing
  taller and denser.
- **Held by Blightborn:** the SAP glows cyan as Blight drinks it; the ring
  becomes crystal pillars. Defense levels show as the pillars growing taller.
- **Attack stages** ([05](05-territory-and-economy.md#resource-hubs)):
  1. *Shielded:* the walls or pillars are whole.
  2. *Exposed:* they crack, and the roots or the lattice show underneath.
  3. *Collapsing:* the pool starts to drain, and its glow flickers.

## Root-knots / Whisper-nodes (nodes)

- **Neutral:** a knot of grey roots breaking the surface, pulsing faintly with the planet's awareness.
- **Captured by Wildborn:** green shoots and moss spread from it. **Glaze** never forms on it.
- **Captured by Blightborn:** it turns to glass from the centre outward, with a whisper-node crystal rising from it.
- **Vulnerable** (from the source pages): the node **flickers**, roots or
  crystals trembling, so players can read the state in the world as well as on the UI.
- **Inaccessible:** the node is dim and still.

## Why the names differ but the rules don't

Each side sees the same structures through its own nature: the planet grows
them, the Murmur glazes over them. **Rules, stats and positions are identical**
(the mirror). Names and looks only tell the story of who holds what, and
they help players read the battlefield at a glance.

## Structures through a match

Structures aren't static. They grow and change with the match, so the
battlefield tells the story of the fight.

| Phase | The Cradle / the Clutch | Heartseeds / Shard Hearts | Sap Wells / Glow Wells |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Stable Flow** | Freshly split, glistening, small | Young: a seedling or a crystal bud | All neutral: open pools ringed by grey roots |
| **2 · Pre-Aggro** | The birth-pool brightens with every conversion | Swelling as the team's evolution grows | First claimed Wells regrow in their side's material |
| **3 · Resource Stage** | The ring of knots thickens; roots or crystal spread outward | Fully grown, heart beating fast. The main siege targets | Defense levels climb: walls and pillars grow tall |
| **4 · Hunt** | The base's edge nodes are open; the oldest knot (the Base Heart) is exposed | Damaged ones show scars; regrown ones are small and fragile | Hubs change hands often; scars of past owners remain |

**Scars remain.** A Hub that changes hands keeps traces of its previous owner:
a crystal pillar half-overgrown with moss, or roots glazed at the tips. By the
end of a long match, every Well shows its history.

## The sound of structures

| Structure | Wildborn sound | Blightborn sound |
|---|---|---|
| Base (idle) | A slow heartbeat from the birth-pool | A deep, steady chime |
| Base (conversion) | A rush like sap rising through a trunk | A rising harmonic, like a glass being rubbed |
| Enemy Core (idle) | A muffled pulse inside the seed husk | A geode humming |
| Enemy Core (under attack) | The pulse quickens | The hum rises in pitch |
| Enemy Core (destroyed) | A dry crack and a long exhale | A shatter, then silence |
| Resource Hub (captured) | Roots groaning as they thicken | Crystal singing as it grows |

A player can tell, by sound alone, that one of their Cores is under attack,
even if they're across the map.

## When a structure falls

- **A Heartseed falls:** its husk splits, the amber heart dims to grey, and the
  roots running back toward the Cradle wither one after another. Players can
  watch the loss travel home.
- **A Shard Heart falls:** the geode shatters outward in a ring of glittering
  dust that drifts on the wind for a long time.
- **A Hub is uprooted:** the pool drains in a spiral, and the walls or pillars
  sink back into the ground.
- **A Base Heart falls:** the match ends ([06](06-match-flow.md#how-a-match-ends)).

## Readability rules for structures

1. **Ownership is always clear:** team outline colours on every captured structure, as with creatures.
2. **Health is visible in the world:** cracks, dimming glow and drooping roots
   show damage without needing a health bar.
3. **Attack stages are distinct:** each Hub stage has a different silhouette
   (whole walls → cracked walls → draining pool), readable at range.
4. **Regrowing structures look fragile:** a regrown Core is visibly small, so
   both teams know it's vulnerable.

## Why the Cradle is a birth place

In the lore, Kith bud from birth-nodes ([12](12-life-on-greyborn.md#the-kith-life-cycle)).
Making each team's base a birth-node cluster ties three systems to one image:
respawning is being budded again, converting cores is feeding the place you
were born, and the Base Heart is the oldest knot, the place the whole brood
came from. Destroying it isn't just winning a match: it's ending a brood's
ability to be reborn.

## Hub Defense levels, shown in the world

Hub Defense Level (1–10, from the source pages) is visible on every Hub, so
players can read it without opening a menu.

| Level | Sap Well (Wildborn) | Glow Well (Blightborn) | Effect (both sides) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A low ring of roots around the pool | A ring of short crystal stubs | Base Hub Health |
| 2–3 | Roots thicken into knee-high walls | Stubs grow into waist-high pillars | +400 Health and +5% ally damage reduction per level |
| 4–5 | Walls of root reach shoulder height; moss covers them | Pillars reach shoulder height and begin to glow | |
| 6–7 | Walls arch over parts of the pool; flowers bloom along them | Pillars lean inward, forming partial arches of crystal | |
| 8–9 | A near-complete dome of living wood | A near-complete cage of crystal | |
| 10 | A full living dome with a single opening, humming with the planet's pulse | A full crystal cage, ringing softly | Maximum Health and protection |

At Level 10, a Hub is unmistakable from anywhere on the map: a dome of trees
or a cage of glass. Teams know at a glance which Hubs will be hard to take.

## The conversion sequence, step by step

Converting cores at the base is the most frequent "big moment" in a match. It should feel ceremonial.

1. **Arrival:** the player reaches the birth-pool. Their carry aura is still humming.
2. **Kneeling:** the creature kneels at the pool's edge. The channel begins (C1/C2/C3 time).
3. **Pouring:** cores flow out of the creature as streams of light (amber or cyan) into the pool.
4. **Rising:** the pool brightens and rises slightly, and its light climbs the creature's body.
5. **Growth:** at the end of the channel, the creature visibly swells: a level-up flash for each level gained.
6. **Stage change** (if reached): the transformation plays immediately, at the pool, where the brood was born.

**Interruption:** if damaged, the channel breaks and the streams of light
snap back into the creature. The cores aren't lost, but the time is.

## Regrowth of Heartseeds and Shard Hearts

When an Enemy Core is destroyed, it can regrow after four minutes if the team
still holds adjacent territory.

- **Heartseed regrowth:** a tiny shoot pushes up through the withered husk,
  then grows over 30 seconds into a small, new seed with a faint heartbeat.
  It's visibly young and fragile, with half the original Health.
- **Shard Heart regrowth:** crystal needles grow up out of the dust and fuse
  into a small geode over 30 seconds, humming faintly.
- **Blocked regrowth:** if the enemy holds the adjacent nodes, the site stays
  dead: a withered husk or a pile of glass dust. The team has to retake the ground first.

## Structures and wildlife

- **Grazers** avoid bases; predators avoid Hubs with high Defense levels.
- **Lumen Bees** (Season 4) visit Sap Wells and Glow Wells alike.
- **Marrowhounds** gather around destroyed Cores, sniffing for dropped cores.
- **Siege Beetles** can be lured into enemy structures and damage them ([09](09-wildlife.md)).
- **Strays** sometimes wander near a team's Cradle or Clutch and stop to watch the birth-pool, curious.

## The structure sound mix

Structures are the background heartbeat of a match. Their sounds should be
present but low, rising only when something happens: an attack on a Core, a
Hub changing hands, a conversion. A player who knows the map should be able
to hear, faintly, the state of the battle from their own base.

## Structure design principles

1. **Structures are alive.** They grow, react, scar and regrow.
2. **Ownership is always clear** through team outline colours.
3. **Health is visible in the world,** not just in bars.
4. **Both sides' structures are mirrors** in rules, sizes and positions.
5. **Every structure has a lore reason to exist:** births, organs, hearts and nerves of the planet.

## Structures in special modes

| Mode | How structures change |
|---|---|
| **Brood Skirmish** | One Hub (the Iris on the Skull Basin) and one Enemy Core per team; bases are smaller |
| **Apex Hunt** | No Enemy Cores; apex lairs replace them as the main objectives |
| **The Truce** | Bases are shared: a **braided Cradle-Clutch** where roots and crystal grow together around one birth-pool (Season 6). Players of both sides convert there |
| **Season finales** | Special structures: the Glass Range's hearts, the Waking's resonance nodes, the Glass Flower, Seedbeds |

The **braided birth-pool** in the Truce is one of the most important images of
Season 6: Wildborn and Blightborn kneeling at the same pool, light of both
colours pouring into it together.

## Structures and the story

Every structure carries a piece of the story:

- **Bases** are where broods are born: the war is fought by the newly born.
- **Heartseeds** are seeds: the planet's future. **Shard Hearts** are geodes:
  the Murmur's crystallised will. Destroying them starves a brood's growth.
- **Wells** are where the planet's blood pools, and the Murmur drinks it.
- **Nodes** are the planet's nerves; Blighting them is how the Murmur learns to feel what the planet feels.

## Common questions

**Why do both sides have the same structures in the same places?** The
mirror rule: rules, stats and positions are always identical. Only material changes.

**Can a structure be "converted" from one side to the other?** Hubs can:
captured Hubs regrow in the new owner's material. Bases and Enemy Cores can't;
they're destroyed, not taken.

**Does the Clamor attack structures?** Yes: Scald eats Hub walls and pillars
alike, and Spore Kites can seed Scald on base edges. It's one more reason both
teams want Landfalls dealt with quickly.

## Structure checklist for new maps

- Two bases at rotationally symmetric positions.
- Five Hubs: two near each base and one central, all equidistant in total from both bases.
- Three Enemy Core sites per team, each with adjacent nodes so regrowth can be fought over.
- Clear sightlines to every Hub's dome or cage from at least one high point.
- No structure placed where a map hazard (lava, tide, ice) could destroy it without player action.
