# 34 — Creatures of the Far Regions

The first 42 creatures ([09](09-wildlife.md)) live mostly in the six core
biomes. Season 4 added four more ([30](30-season-4.md#new-creatures-for-bloom)).
This chapter adds **natives** for the regions that so far only had variants:
the **Rimewastes, Cinderveil, Shattered Coast** and **Heartwood**. Same rules

## Notes

- **Two winged creatures** (Ash Vulture, Shard Gull) count as two-legged, in
  keeping with the director's body plans. Greyborn has birds, but no insects
  are ridden and nothing flies higher than tree height. The skies belong to the Fall-line.
- **The Heartwood Elk** is the first **apex that can't be harmed.** It isn't a
  threat; it's a blessing that both teams want to be standing near. In lore,
  it's the closest thing the planet has to a voice.
- **Stillheart affinity** (Hoarfrost Mites) is the first creature tied to the seventh lineage.

## The creatures, described

**Drift Owlbear (#47).** In the Rimewastes, some snowdrifts are too round and
too regular. Under each one sleeps a Drift Owlbear: a huge, white-furred
creature with a hooked beak and the shoulders of a bear. It sleeps through
most of the year, and its breath forms a faint plume of frost above the drift.
Step on it and it rises in an explosion of snow, roaring, and fights anything
in reach. Experienced players learn to read the plumes and walk around them,
or to lure enemies across them.

**Hoarfrost Mites (#48).** Tiny frost spiders, each smaller than a fingernail,
that live in their thousands in the cracks of the ice. Where they gather, they
spin frost rather than silk, and the ground turns slick and white. They're
the only creature with an affinity to Stillheart: they swarm toward it, and
the Stillheart's frost seems to feed them.

**Cinder Salamander (#49).** A slick black lizard with glowing orange veins
that swims through lava as easily as a fish through water. It surfaces to
bask on cooling rock, and dives when anything approaches. When it dies in the
lava, its body cools instantly into a flat black stone, a stepping stone
across the channel, which can open a shortcut on volcanic maps.

**Ash Vulture (#50).** Grey-feathered scavengers with bald red heads that ride
the hot air above the Cinderveil. They can sense death before it happens,
gathering in slow circles above fights. Players learn to look up: circling
vultures mean a fight is happening, or about to. They land only after the
fight is over, to pick at what's left, including any dropped cores.

**Tidal Strider (#51).** A long-legged, six-legged creature that walks on the
surface of the sea, its wide feet spreading its weight like a water insect.
It wades across the flooded causeways of the Shattered Coast at high tide,
and players can move beneath it, sheltered and hidden, while the tide is in.

**Shard Gull (#52).** A noisy coastal bird that collects glittering meteor
glass for its nest. Flocks of them dive into the shard-studded surf and come
up with fragments in their beaks. Killing one drops a small Starshard worth
bonus cores, which makes them a tempting distraction during a fight.

**Heartwood Elk (#53).** The largest creature in the Heartwood, and the
gentlest. It stands taller than a Stage 3 Titan, with antlers so vast they
branch like a tree and carry real leaves and flowers. It moves without hurry
and nothing can harm it. Where it walks, the ground warms and roots spread.
It appears rarely in a map's Heartwood grove: a sign that the planet is
paying attention. Players of both sides stop to look at it.

**Amber Beetles (#54).** Small, slow beetles that carry a single bead of SAP on
their backs like a jewel. They gather around the Amber Lakes and the planet's
groves. Killing one gives SAP to the team instead of cores, a small but
welcome boost to the team's economy.

## Why these regions needed natives

Before this chapter, the outer regions had only variants of creatures from
the core biomes. A world feels thin if its strangest places have nothing of
their own. Each far region now has creatures that could live nowhere else:
a bear that sleeps in snowdrifts, a lizard that swims in lava, a strider that
walks on the sea, and an elk so close to the planet's heart that the Murmur
has never touched one.

## How they fit the food web

- **Ash Vultures** are scavengers, joining Marrowhounds at the end of the chain ([15](15-ecology.md)).
- **Drift Owlbears** are apex-like predators of the Rimewastes, hunting Snowback Grazers and Rimeback Stags.
- **Cinder Salamanders** eat insects drawn to the heat; Ash Vultures sometimes catch them.
- **Shard Gulls** eat Tidecrawlers and small sea creatures, and collect glass.
- **Amber Beetles** feed on SAP and are eaten by Kith and grazers alike.
- **The Heartwood Elk** eats nothing anyone has seen. It may drink only SAP.

## The far regions as habitats

**The Rimewastes** support only the hardiest life: animals with thick fur,
slow hearts and long sleeps. Everything here waits out the cold. The Drift
Owlbears, Hoarfrost Mites, Snowback Grazers and Frostfangs all share one
trait: they move slowly most of the time and very fast when they must, like
Stillheart, the lineage that comes from this land.

**The Cinderveil** is the opposite: life that loves heat. Salamanders swim in
lava; vultures ride the rising air; Ashcap mushrooms grow in fresh ash.
Nothing lives here comfortably, but some creatures live here well.

**The Shattered Coast** is life at the edge of two worlds: the planet's land
and its sea, the planet's life and the Murmur's glass. Gulls collect the
Murmur's shards, Striders walk the shallows, and the whales sing the Blight still.

**The Heartwood** is life at its most peaceful. The Elk and the Amber Beetles
are gentle, and nothing here hunts openly. It is the only place on Greyborn
where predators seem to rest.

## Closing note

The far regions are where Greyborn feels most like a whole planet: places
most matches never visit, full of creatures that belong only there. Each one
should make a player who glimpses it wonder what else lives beyond the edge of the map.
