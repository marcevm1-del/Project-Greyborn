# 39 — World Events

Short, recurring events that make the world feel alive between the big
season beats. Each is a **weekend or 3-day event** that changes atmosphere,
wildlife or rewards, **never the balance between sides**. All are proposals.

## Rules for world events

1. **Fair to both sides.** Every change applies equally to Wildborn and Blightborn.
2. **Readable.** Every event shows a clear sky or weather change at match start, so players know it's on.
3. **Rooted in the world.** Every event is something that would really happen on Greyborn: a migration, a storm, a song.
4. **Not in ranked** by default, except purely visual events, to keep competitive play consistent.
5. **Front points** ([18](18-the-answering-war.md)) still count during events, so the war keeps moving.

## The events in depth

Each event should feel like a natural happening on a living planet, not a
playlist rule. Here is what each one looks, sounds and feels like.

**The Great Migration.** For a weekend, every open map is crossed by herds far
bigger than usual. On Ashfall Crossing the migration runs twice as often; on
the Ash Sea and the Ashen Steppe maps, smaller herds cut across every route.
The ground rumbles constantly. Mossback Grazers, Rimeback Stags and Stiltwalkers
move together, and predators (Ashfang packs) shadow them on the flanks. Players
learn to fight *around* the herds: a stampede can split a team, cover a
retreat, or carry a careless Base Form away. The event ends with a Memory
unlock showing the herds following the Hunt That Never Ended.

**Starfall Nights.** Every match is played at night under a sky full of
falling meteor glass. Shard Storms are frequent, and every falling shard
lights the ground cyan-violet for a moment before it fades. Starshard veins
give double cores, so the crater zones become the busiest places on the map.
The music is quieter and stranger: glass tones over a slow heartbeat. Players
remember Starfall weekends for their look: dark maps lit only by the Lantern
moon, Blight's glow and falling stars.

**Moth Bloom.** Dusk on every map. As the light fades, clouds of Ember Moths
rise from the grass in slow spirals. They're harmless and easy to eat, but
anyone standing inside a cloud is outlined in soft gold and revealed. It's a
gentle event with a hidden edge: the most beautiful places on the map are
also the most exposed.

**Calving Days.** Mossback Calves appear on every map, tottering after their
herds. Harming one turns the whole herd territorial for a minute. The event
asks players to be careful in a world that is usually only a battlefield.
Wildborn and Blightborn players alike earn the event reward for *not* harming
calves across a set number of matches. It's the only event that rewards restraint.

**Sap Rain.** Warm amber rain falls on every map. SAP income rises by 20% for
both sides, roots spread a little faster in the wet soil, and every surface
glistens. The rain makes Blight look different too: crystal streaked with
running sap, which artists can use as one of the rare images of the two
materials touching peacefully.

**The Long Cold.** Rime Fog and frost on every map. Rimewastes variants
(Frostfangs, Snowback Grazers, Rime Weavers) appear everywhere, and creatures
respawn more slowly. The soundscape drops to near-silence, with ice creaking
and the Sleeper's slow heartbeat underneath. Fights feel slower and tenser.

**Whale Song.** On coastal maps, Stillsong Whales surface every four minutes
and sing. While they sing, Blight stops spreading for 20 seconds, and the
sea goes perfectly still. Everywhere else, the song can be heard faintly on
the wind. The event is a reminder that the planet has old, gentle defences.

**Fever Storms.** Heat haze, red skies at dusk, and predators that attack
more readily. Cinderveil variants appear on every map, and Embergrass fires
spread farther. The event shows the planet's fighting mood: everything is
louder, hotter and more dangerous.

**The Quiet Hours.** Hush Cicada colonies on every map halve all sound
signatures. It's a weekend for ambushes and careful movement. Hollow and
Thornrunner players love it; Titan players learn patience.

**Hunt Weekend.** An apex creature appears in every match, one per phase.
Matches become three-way struggles: the two broods and a Greyback Colossus,
Hive Colossus, Eightfold Matron or Sixfold Wyrm. Apex kills give a unique
trophy mark.

**Landfall Rush.** From Season 3 onward, two Clamor Landfalls per match. The
sky streaks red, Scald spreads from two points, and both teams have to decide
how much to fight the Clamor and how much to fight each other.

## How events are announced

1. **In the world first.** The day before an event, a hint appears on every
   map: distant rumbling before the Migration, red streaks before Landfall
   Rush, a single falling star before Starfall Nights.
2. **On the War Map.** The event shows as weather across the Greyreach.
3. **At match start.** The sky and weather change is clear in the first
   seconds, with a short HUD banner naming the event.

## Checklist for designing new events

- Is it something that would really happen on Greyborn?
- Does it change the same thing for both sides?
- Can a player see it from the sky or weather at match start?
- Does it give a reason to play differently, not just a reward?
- Does it connect to a Turn, a Memory or a season story?
