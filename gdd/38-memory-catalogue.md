## Memory design guide

Every Memory follows the same grammar so they feel like one collection:

1. **Length:** 5–8 seconds. Never longer.
2. **No words, no text, no UI** during the vision.
3. **One clear image** at its heart: the thing a player will remember.
4. **Sound first:** each Memory opens on a sound before the image fades in.
5. **A slight dreamlike treatment:** soft edges, a little colour drift toward amber (the planet's memories) or cyan (the Murmur's).
6. **Whose memory is it?** Every Memory belongs to the planet or the Murmur, and the colour drift shows which.
7. **Ends on stillness:** the last half-second holds on a still image before fading.

## How Memories unlock in the Codex

- **Map Memories** unlock the first time a player touches the site (a 2-second channel outside combat).
- **Season Memories** unlock for everyone at the right week of the season.
- **Finale Memories** unlock for players who take part in the finale; the result Memory unlocks for everyone.
- **Encounter Memories** unlock on first defeat of a Hushed Answer.
- **Hidden Memories** (proposal): a few Memories appear only at a certain
  time of day, Turn or weather, such as *Moth Bloom* at dusk or *The Planet
  Sleeps* in Rime, to reward players who explore.

## Memories as a story map

Read in order, the Codex pages tell the whole history of Greyborn:
before the Fall (Page 1), the Fall (2), the First Answers (3), the Hushed (4),
the Long Stand (5), the Sleeper (6), the Second Light (7), the Glass Flower
(8), everyday life (9), the Quiet World (10), the creatures (11), the Old Ones
(12) and the defeated Hushed Answers (13). A player who completes the Codex
has seen Greyborn's whole story without a single word.

## Choosing what becomes a Memory

Not everything deserves a Memory. A good Memory candidate is:

- A moment the **planet or the Murmur would remember**.
- **Visual** enough to work in eight seconds without words.
- **Connected** to a place players can visit.
- **Emotional**: wonder, grief, fear, tenderness or awe.

## The Memory most players will remember

If one Memory had to stand for all of them, it would be **#40, Return**: an
Ascendant's body sinking slowly back into the soil after a battle, and a
flower opening where it lay. It's found on any map, it's quiet, and it holds
the whole idea of the planet in eight seconds.
