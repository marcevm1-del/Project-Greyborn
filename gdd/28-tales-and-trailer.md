# 28 — Tales of Greyborn & Announcement Trailer

Greyborn's **world** has no words (decided). These prose pieces are for the
**team and for pitching**: they set the tone for writers, artists and
audio. They are not in-game text. All content is a proposal.

## Tale 1 · Budding

The knot had been swelling for nine days. On the tenth, it split.

The first thing the Kith knew was cold air. The second was its brood: three
others, wet and grey, blinking beside it in the root-light. The third was
hunger, and under the hunger something else, something vast and slow,
pressing up through the soil like warmth through a stone.

*Go,* said the ground, without saying anything.

The four of them went. Past the Mossbacks grazing in the mist, past a
Glimmerfox that vanished when they looked at it. By dusk the Kith had eaten
its first Sapbloom and caught its first skink, and something in its back had
begun to ache, the way a branch must ache before it grows.

Far to the north, a scar in the sky began to glow. The Kith turned to look
at it, and didn't know why its fur stood on end.

## Tale 2 · The Whisper

This knot had been swelling too. But the soil around it had turned to glass.

The Kith that stepped out didn't feel cold air. It felt **company**: a
thousand voices, all speaking at once and all speaking the same thing,
softly, gently, from everywhere.

*There you are,* said the voices. *We've been waiting. Look how well you're made.*

Its brood stepped out beside it, and they turned their heads at the same
moment, in perfect step, and none of them found that strange.

To the south, past the glass, a forest breathed in and out. The voices
wanted it very much. The Kith found that it wanted it too.

## Tale 3 · Stillsong

The whale had sung the same song for four hundred years. It was a simple
song. It told the sea to be still, and the sea listened.

When the first stone fell, the whale had watched the light cross the sky
and had sung louder, and the glitter in the water near the cliffs had
stopped spreading where its song reached.

Now the whale watched a **second** light, small and red, moving slowly along
the old scar in the sky. It didn't look like the first one. It looked
angrier. The whale listened to it for a long time.

Then it sang louder.

## Tale 4 · Side by Side

The Titan had fought glass creatures since the day it awakened. It knew the
ring of them, the cold of them, the way they moved together like one thing.
It had never stood beside one.

Now the sky was red, and the ground shook with a roaring that came from
everywhere. Out of the smoke came the burning things, screaming, and the
Titan raised its arm to slam, and saw, on its left, a Blightborn Brawler
crouched to roll.

They looked at each other. The whispering around the Brawler was quiet, for
once. Afraid.

The Titan slammed. The Brawler rolled. The burning thing in front of them
flew apart, and where the Titan's roots had cracked the ground, the Brawler's
crystal grew into the cracks, and the two held, woven, against the fire.

Neither of them understood what they had done. But the ground under their
feet remembered.

## Tale 5 · The Last Braid

After the fever, after the burning, there was one place left.

It was small: a patch of shoreline no wider than a Titan's stride, where a
root and a crystal had grown so tightly together that the fire could not find
where one ended and the other began. The planet's heat had passed over it.
The Murmur's cold had passed over it. Neither had taken it back.

A Stray found it one evening. It sniffed the root, which smelled of soil and
sap. It sniffed the crystal, which hummed very faintly, a sound like a song
someone had stopped singing. The Stray lay down between them and slept.

In the morning it was still there, and so was the Braid.

## Tale 6 · Waking

For a very long time, there had been nothing but cold and the slow sound of
its own heart.

Then something cracked.

Light came down through the ice in a long blue line. Voices came with it,
two kinds. One was warm and deep and came from underneath, from everywhere,
and it felt like being held. The other came from above, many voices speaking
together, curious, eager, reaching.

The Sleeper opened its eyes. Above it, small shapes stood on the ice: little
grey Kith on one side, little glass Kith on the other, all of them perfectly
still, all of them looking down.

It didn't know what it was for. Not yet. It only knew that the warm voice was
sorry, very sorry, and that the eager voice wanted to know its shape.

The ice broke, and Stillheart rose into the cold air, and breathed out frost.

## Tale 7 · The Gull and the Red Star

The Shard Gull had collected glass all its life: bright pieces from the surf,
cyan and violet, for its nest on the cliffs. It knew every colour of glass the
sea gave up.

One night, a new colour crossed the sky. Red, like an ember. The gull watched
it from its nest. It did not want that colour in its nest.

The red thing fell into the reef with a sound that shook every nest on the
cliffs, and steam rose from the sea for a long time. In the morning, the surf
brought up new pieces: black, crusted, still warm.

The gull left them on the sand.

## Tale 8 · The Hum

The Hushed Kith had never made a sound of its own. It had chirped when the
voices wanted it to chirp, and been still when they wanted it still, and it
had never minded, because the voices were always there.

Then one evening, at the edge of the glass, something came out of it that the
voices hadn't given it. Four notes. Up, up, down, down.

It stopped, surprised. The brood around it had stopped too. Then, one by one,
they made the same four notes, and the voices, for once, were quiet, as if
listening.

Far to the north, in the crystal sea, the light moved through the spires in
the same four-note rhythm, and kept moving long after the brood had gone to sleep.

## Tale 9 · Greymother

She had planted the first of the Rootwilds. She remembered when there was
nothing there but grey stone and a single root.

Now she walked the edge of the Heartwood, very slowly, a few steps a day.
Mossbacks grazed in her shade. Birds nested in her antlers, which had long
since become a tree.

She had felt the stone fall from the sky. She had felt the glass creep in
from the north. She had felt the planet call new broods, and send them north,
and take their bodies back into the soil when they fell.

She did not go north. That was not what she was for, anymore.

Instead, every time a brood of young Kith passed beneath her on their way to
the war, she lowered her great head, very slightly, as if in blessing, and let
a few seeds fall from her antlers onto the path behind them.

## Writing tales for Greyborn: guidance

These tales aren't in the game; they're for the team and for marketing. They
follow a few rules so they stay true to the world:

1. **Animal perspectives only.** Tales are told from a creature's point of
   view (a Kith, a gull, a whale, an Old One), never a person's.
2. **Sensory, not explained.** Describe what the creature sees, hears and feels.
   Never explain the lore directly.
3. **The minds stay vast.** The planet and the Murmur appear as feelings, pressures, voices and light, never as characters who speak in sentences.
   (Tale 2 and Tale 6 give the Murmur's voices words only as the creature *feels* them.)
4. **End on an image.** Every tale ends on a single still image.
5. **Short.** Each tale should read in under two minutes.

## Uses for the tales and trailers

- **Pitch:** the tales set the tone for publishers and partners.
- **Team alignment:** everyone on the team reads them before working on a new area.
- **Marketing:** short tales on the store page and social media; trailers at season launches.
- **Loading screens:** single lines from tales, shown without attribution, as the only "text" a player might see in the world (optional, director's call).
