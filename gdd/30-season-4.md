## The central mystery

The Murmur can only copy (decided lore, [24](24-the-three-minds.md)). Every
Blighted thing is a glass version of something from Greyborn. But the Glass
Flower isn't a copy of anything on this planet.

**Decided (2026-10-02): the Murmur slowly learns to create.** The Glass
Flower is its **first creation**, and it is shaped like a flower from the
Murmur's **lost home world** (the backstory is also decided; see
[37](37-the-clamor.md#the-clamor-and-the-murmur-what-really-happened)). The
Murmur made something new for the first time, and what it made was a memory of home.

Players don't learn this all at once. The two "explanations" are the two
halves of the truth:

1. **A shape from far away:** the planet recognises it as foreign, a flower from another world.
2. **The first made thing:** the Murmur didn't copy it from anything here. It made it.

The season finale's community result decides **which half the story reveals
first**. Season 5 reveals the other half ([47](47-season-5.md)).

## New map: The Nerve (Underroot)

**Identity:** a three-level cave map along the planet's nerves, where light
gives you away.

- **Three levels** connected by root-ramps and drop-shafts. Fights happen
  above and below each other.
- **Nerve pulses:** every **20 s**, a wave of amber light runs along the
  nerve-roots in the walls. Any player in a corridor when a pulse passes is
  **revealed** for 2 s. Timing your moves between pulses is the map's skill.
- **Crater zone:** *The Blight Pocket*, where the Murmur has crept in through the tunnels.
- **Heartwood grove:** *The Root Tendril*, a single strand of the First Root reaching up from far below.
- **Memory site:** *The Quiet Descends*, the Hollow First Answer sealing the deep places.
- **Wildlife:** Lantern Lizards and Glow Lizards, Glasslegs, Burrowtusks (new tunnels mid-match), Lanternmoss.
- **Apex:** the **Sixfold Wyrm**, which tunnels between levels.
- **Favours:** Hollow (blink between levels), Thornrunner (ambush), Stillheart (holding chokepoints).

## Finale mode: The Glass Flower

- **Map:** a special version of **Breathing Canopy**, with the Glass Flower
  growing in the centre glade.
- **The flower grows** during the match, spreading glass petals across nearby nodes.
- **Objective (same for both sides):** hold the glade to **listen** to the
  flower. While a team holds it, its **Understanding** bar fills.
  - Wildborn listening is shown as roots reaching toward the flower.
  - Blightborn listening is shown as the whispers growing quieter around it.
- **Winning a match:** more Understanding at 12 minutes.
- **Community result:**
  - **Wildborn understand it first:** the planet touches the flower and
    recognises something *foreign*, a shape from far away. The story tilts toward
    **explanation 1** (a memory of the Murmur's home).
  - **Blightborn understand it first:** the whispers fall silent around the
    flower for the first time ever, as if the Murmur itself is surprised. The
    story tilts toward **explanation 2** (the Murmur's first creation).
- Both results unlock the same **cosmetic set** (a glass-and-flower theme) for everyone.

## How Season 4 feels

After three seasons of dread (a glass mountain waking, a frozen giant, a
burning second stone), Season 4 opens in Bloom, and the world exhales. The
light is brighter, flowers are everywhere, calves stumble after their herds,
and wild Kith broods play in every clearing. For a few weeks, Greyborn
feels like a world at peace, even though the war goes on in every match.

Then something glitters in the Glasswaste that shouldn't be there.

The mood of Season 4 is **wonder**. It's the first time the story asks
players to be curious rather than afraid. The Glass Flower isn't a threat. It
doesn't attack. It just *is*: strange, beautiful, and impossible. Players of
both sides gather around it in the finale not to destroy it, but to listen.

## The Glass Flower, described

It grows alone in a field of black glass, as tall as a Stage 2 creature. Its
stem is a single spiral of crystal, slowly turning. Its petals are thin as
ice and curl in a shape no Greyborn plant has ever had: five long petals that
fold back on themselves like hands opening. At night it glows a soft, pale
blue, not the Murmur's usual cyan-violet. At dawn, it closes.

When anything comes near, it makes a sound: a single high tone, steady and
pure, unlike any whisper or chime the Murmur has made before. The Blighted
creatures around it stand perfectly still and face it, as if they've never
seen anything like it either.

**Art note:** the Flower must look **foreign** even to players who know the
Murmur's look well. Its pale blue light, spiral stem and folding petals come
from the Quiet World ([48](48-beyond-the-sky.md)), not from Greyborn.

## A moment on The Nerve

Level two of The Nerve. A Wildborn Hollow waits at a corridor junction in the
dark, counting. A pulse of amber light races along the nerve-roots in the
walls toward it, and it slips into a side tunnel just before the light
passes. Behind it, two Blightborn caught in the corridor flash bright for two
seconds, revealed to everyone. The Hollow's Thornrunner partner is already
dropping down the shaft from level one, toward the light.

Twenty seconds later, the next pulse comes, from the other direction.

- **More life:** extra grazers, calves and Strays on every map.
- **More healing:** Sapbloom and Heartbloom are common.
- **Brighter light:** the brightest season visually, which makes the Flower's pale blue glow stand out even more.

- **The turning point** (Season 5, [47](47-season-5.md)): the full truth of the Flower.

## Lumen Bees and Seedcallers in matches

The two key Bloom creatures change how maps play this season.

**Lumen Bees** drift in golden swarms between flowers and nodes. A node they
visit gives +25% SAP for 60 seconds, so both teams want the bees on their
nodes. Wildborn protect swarms; Blightborn can infect them or destroy them.
A swarm is fragile, so the fight is about **positioning**: escorting a swarm
to your node while keeping enemies away from it.

**Seedcallers** are slender, shy, two-legged creatures that plant seeds as they
walk. Wherever they pass, neutral cells slowly regrow, even burnt-out Scald.
They don't belong to either side. On a map where the Clamor has struck, a
Seedcaller's path is the fastest way for the land to recover. Wildborn can
rally them; Blightborn can try to destroy their seedlings.

## The finale, walked through

Breathing Canopy, special version. In the centre glade stands the Glass
Flower, glowing pale blue, its high tone audible across the map.

**0:00–3:00.** Both broods bud and race for the glade. The canopy is up; the
light is bright. The first team to reach the glade begins to fill its
Understanding bar, and the Flower's petals turn slightly toward them.

**3:00–6:00.** The canopy falls. In the dark, the glade becomes an ambush
point. Teams fight for control of it in short, sharp clashes. Glass petals
spread across the nodes around it as the Flower grows.

**6:00–9:00.** The Flower is now the size of a Stage 3 creature. Its tone
deepens. Holding the glade fills Understanding faster, so the fights grow
more desperate.

**9:00–12:00.** The final minutes. The canopy rises one last time and the
whole glade is lit. Both teams commit everything. At 12:00, the Flower opens
fully, and the team with more Understanding hears it first: either the
planet's roots flinching from something foreign, or the whispers going silent.

Then the sides swap, and the match is played again.

## Why the season is about listening

Every other finale asks players to fight something or escort something.
Season 4 asks them to **listen**. The Understanding bar fills not by killing,
but by holding still around the Flower. It's a deliberate change of pace,
and it matches what the story is about: both minds encountering something
they don't understand, and trying to.

## Seeds of Season 5 in Season 4

Season 4 quietly prepares the turning point:

- **Seedcallers** show players that the world can regrow, a theme Season 5's Seedbeds make playable.
- **The Flower's tone** is the first sound the Murmur makes that isn't copied. In
  Season 5, the Hushed Kith begin to hum a tune built on that same tone.
- **The Blighted creatures' stillness** around the Flower hints that even the
  Murmur's own creatures are surprised by it, the first sign that the Murmur is changing.
- **Lumen Bees** pollinate both rooted and blighted nodes without preference.
  It's a small detail that later supports braided ground: nature doesn't take sides.

## How the community result is presented

At the end of week 10, the result is revealed in the same way to every player:

1. The War Map darkens, and the Glasswaste glows.
2. A short wordless cinematic plays: the Flower opening fully at night.
3. Depending on the result, the camera either follows a root reaching toward
   the Flower and flinching, or pulls back as the whispers around it fall silent.
4. The new Memory unlocks for everyone, and the Glass-and-flower set is granted.
5. The War Map's history layer ([18](18-the-answering-war.md#the-map-remembers)) records the result forever.
